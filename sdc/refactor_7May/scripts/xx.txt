Here's a talk track, one beat per chart, pitched so the non-technical people in the room follow it and the technical ones don't get bored.

**Opening (30 seconds).** Three colleagues played suppliers. Each ran the happy path on 26 August, then a deliberate-failure path a week later where they submitted bad data and had to fix it. That's six sessions, and the same three people in both, which matters for how much we can claim. What we were looking for: can a supplier get in, fill the template, upload, understand where they stand, and recover from a mistake without calling us.

**Chart 1: the scorecard.** Read it top to bottom as the supplier's journey. The top is clean: every tester got the email, clicked through, found the task, both rounds, no exceptions. The rework path is the other big win: when they submitted bad data, all three noticed the change request on their own, all three got a clear error message, all three re-uploaded successfully. That's the part of the process we were most worried about and it held. The problems are in the middle and the end: the Excel template itself, what the status screen shows after upload, and knowing when you're finished. So the shape of the story is "getting in and recovering works; the template and the sense of closure don't yet."

**Chart 2: the grid.** This is the same data one question at a time, for whoever will fix things. Point at the colors, not the rows: green at the top, orange concentrated in three rows. Dates and amounts don't display cleanly in the template. The confirmation screen shows internal codes and row-failure counts to suppliers, all three testers in round two. And "was it clear you were done?" fails for two of three in round two. Then say the caveat yourself before someone else does: the "instructions clear" row goes from one green to three greens, but that's the same people doing it a second time, so it's learning, not a fix.

**Chart 3: what they wrote.** Thirty distinct issues from six comment boxes. The two biggest themes, formats and template wording, are cheap: they're header text, date-format guidance, and a currency dropdown that rejects its own values. The ones I'd flag as urgent are smaller on the chart but worse in kind. Links and login: two testers found that the confirmation email links to a Workato login page suppliers can't use, and one couldn't get a password-reset email at all. Those stop a real supplier cold. Validation gaps: the template accepted an exit date before a start date and a "leaver" with a future start date. That's bad data arriving silently, which is the expensive kind.

**Closing.** If you want a prioritization frame that a mixed audience will accept: fix first what blocks a supplier (links, password reset), then what lets bad data through quietly (date conversion, missing validation rules), then what generates support tickets (status wording, the rework email, the completion screen). Péter's comment is a good last line, roughly: he can already foresee suppliers coming back asking what exactly they need to correct, and us explaining that they have to download the report first.

Two things to not overclaim: three people, all internal, one of whom said himself he might only be confused because he isn't a supplier. This tells you where to look, not how often real suppliers will hit each problem.
